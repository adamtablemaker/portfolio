Modular Fabrication Systems

Bridging Computational Design and Physical Construction

This research investigates a modular construction system that combines standardized structural materials with digitally fabricated components. Rather than designing individual products, the project develops a reusable construction language capable of producing adaptable physical systems through computational design, additive manufacturing, and rapid prototyping.

Design Philosophy

The project begins by rethinking furniture as a modular platform rather than a single finished object. Standardized materials remain constant while custom fabricated components provide adaptability, repairability, and rapid reconfiguration for new applications.

Material Strategy

Readily available Schedule 40 PVC tubing forms the structural framework while custom printed interfaces provide geometry-specific functionality. The hybrid approach combines accessibility, repeatability, and computational flexibility.

Prototype Development

The floor lamp demonstrates the modular system as a proof of concept. A small family of reusable joints generates a complete structural assembly, illustrating how digital design can produce adaptable physical products.

Connection System

The modular connection system is the foundation of the research. Rather than requiring custom structural members, engineered adapters transform standardized pipe into a configurable building system. Compression fits, sleeves, O-rings, and interchangeable printed components allow assemblies to be repeatedly built, modified, repaired, and expanded without redesigning the entire structure.

Digital Fabrication

Each component was developed through an iterative digital workflow. CAD models were optimized for additive manufacturing, balancing print orientation, material usage, tolerances, and assembly requirements. Rapid prototyping allowed designs to be evaluated and refined before physical implementation.

Future Development

While demonstrated through a modular lighting system, the fabrication strategy extends far beyond furniture. Future applications include exhibition systems, deployable structures, research equipment, adaptive interiors, educational kits, and lightweight architectural assemblies. The project establishes a scalable construction methodology where computational design directly informs physical fabrication.
